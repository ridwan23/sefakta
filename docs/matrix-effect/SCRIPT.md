# SCRIPT: The Matrix Effect (Why Time Slows Down When You Fall)

## Facts & Scientific Source
- **Core Mystery:** Why subjective time seems to dilate or slow down during mortal danger, car crashes, or free falls.
- **The Experiment (2007):** Neuroscientist David Eagleman at Baylor College of Medicine conducted a famous study using a 150-foot suspended amusement drop tower (SCAD diving).
- **The Test:** 23 volunteers wore digital chronometers on their wrists ("perceptual chronometers") that flashed alternating digits too fast to perceive at normal frame rates.
- **The Discovery:** If the brain actually perceived higher temporal resolution (like an ultra-high FPS camera), the jumpers would be able to read the numbers mid-fall. However, none of the jumpers could read the flashing digits.
- **The Mechanism:** Time doesn't slow down in real-time. Instead, the **amygdala** (the brain's emotional threat radar) kicks into overdrive, activating secondary memory pathways. It records a massive surge of dense, sensory details.
- **The Illusion:** Retrospective time dilation. When the memory is recalled later, the massive file size and density of details tricks the conscious mind into believing the event took 3x to 5x longer than it actually did.
